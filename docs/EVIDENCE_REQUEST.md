# Evidence request — asset register and floor space

**For:** whoever holds YBI's fixed asset records and its facility/lease records
**Purpose:** the two blocking gaps in the 2025 indirect cost rate build
**Prepared:** September 2026

These are the two items that move the rate most and that nothing else can
substitute for. Everything else in the build can proceed without them; the rate
itself cannot be sealed.

---

## Why these two

Depreciation and occupancy are together roughly **$1.5M of 2025 cost** against
$6.74M of total expense. YBI is, financially, a real estate operation with
programs attached — gross property and equipment of $23.2M against annual
expenses a third of that size. So the two questions "how much of this building
did the government already pay for" and "who actually uses the space" decide the
rate more than every classification judgment combined.

Modelled range on what we have today is **36% – 46%**. The spread is almost
entirely these two items.

| 1505 Construction in progress (not yet in service) | 438,355.19 |
| **2025 depreciation expense (account 5010)** | **850,382.89** |

**Tech Block Building 5 alone is 38.5% of the depreciable basis.** If it was
publicly funded, roughly $327,000 of 2025 depreciation is unallowable and the
indirect rate falls about 2.9 points. That single asset is the largest open
question in the entire rate.

### What we need, per asset

This list is not ours — it is **2 CFR 200.313(d)(1)**, which sets out what
property records must contain. An asset register that has these fields is
compliant; one that does not is a finding waiting to happen, independently of
the rate work.

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Description and serial or ID number | 200.313(d)(1) |
| **Source of funding, including the FAIN** | 200.313(d)(1) — the field that decides allowability |
| **Percentage of federal participation** in the cost | 200.313(d)(1); 200.436 disallows depreciation on the federal share |
| Who holds title | 200.313(d)(1); federally titled property has disposition conditions |
| Acquisition date and **original acquisition cost** | See the warning below |
| Location — which building, which floor | Drives the occupancy carve-out |
| Use and condition; in service or not | 200.436 requires the asset to be in use |
| Useful life, method, salvage | Consistency across the register |
| Accumulated depreciation at 1 Jan 2025 and 31 Dec 2025 | Ties to the ledger |
| Current-year depreciation | Must reconcile to $850,382.89 |
| Disposal date and proceeds, if disposed | 200.313(e) |
| Date of last physical inventory | 200.313(d)(2) — required at least every two years |

### One warning that matters more than the rest

**Finding 2024-001 says capital reimbursements were netted against asset cost.**
The gross basis on the books is therefore understated, and the federal
participation percentage cannot be derived from what is currently recorded.

So we need **original acquisition cost before any reimbursement was netted**,
plus the journal entries that did the netting, so the gross basis can be
restored and the federal share stated separately. Rebuilding the register from
the current book values would bake the finding into the rate.

### Funding sources to trace specifically

We already know federal and state money is in these buildings. Each needs its
award document and the amount applied to which asset:

- **U.S. EDA**, Economic Adjustment Assistance (ALN 11.307) — $1,872,050
  expended in FY2024. The balance sheet still carries `1120 A/R EDA Grant —
  Federal`, so the relationship is live.
- **Appalachian Regional Commission** (ALN 23.002) — roughly $750,000 direct in
  FY2024.
- **Vindicator Building renovation — $3,000,000**, repayable if the building is
  not used for its intended purpose for twenty years after being placed in
  service (2017).
- **Ohio Board of Regents — $1,500,000** through Youngstown State University
  under a twenty-year joint use agreement, with prorated repayment if YSU's
  right of use ends early.
- Any Ohio Third Frontier, JobsOhio, City of Youngstown or foundation capital
  grants.

### Also needed alongside it

- **Debt schedule tied to specific assets.** `2004 5th Bldg Loan` at $772,315
  and the $804,134 mortgage collateralised by "buildings and rent assignments".
  Interest on debt to acquire or improve buildings is conditionally allowable
  under **2 CFR 200.449**, but only if it can be tied to the asset and the
  financing tested against the conditions there. Untied, the whole $49,001 of
  2025 interest stays out of the pool.
- **Most recent physical inventory count sheets**, whatever form they are in.

### Acceptable formats

Whatever exists. A spreadsheet export from the depreciation software is ideal;
a PDF depreciation schedule works; a fixed asset listing with the funding column
blank is still useful because we can work the funding question separately
against the award documents. **Please do not build anything new** — send what
is on hand and we will tell you what is missing.

### What it unlocks

- Allowable depreciation, per asset, rather than a modelled assumption
- The interest question under 200.449
- Resolution of the largest single open item in the rate
- A property record that satisfies 200.313(d) on its own merits

## 2. Floor space

### Where this stands

Management's estimate is **50% tenant, 30% program, 20% administrative**. That
is a reasonable starting point and it is what the current model uses. It is not
yet evidence, and it has one structural problem: it is a single
organisation-wide percentage.

YBI runs at least six distinct spaces, and they are not used alike. The rent
lines in the 2025 P&L name them:

| Account | 2025 |
|---|---:|
| 4021 TTC Rent | 237,081.48 |
| 4033 Tech Bldg 5 Rent | 126,129.84 |
| 4022 Semple Rent | 109,932.32 |
| **4026 NAMII Rent — Boardman St.** | **108,000.00** |
| 4020 Rent — YBI Main | 56,786.57 |
| 4038 Brite Rent | 632.00 |
| **Total rental income** | **638,862.21** |

A building that is 90% leased to tenants and one that houses the additive
manufacturing lab cannot share one percentage. **The allocation needs to be per
facility**, which is also what the Foundation architecture requires: "Facility
allocation is generally facility-specific, not one organization-wide
percentage."

### What we need, per building

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Total usable square footage** | Measured, ideally BOMA or the figure used for leasing |
| **Common area square footage** | Corridors, restrooms, lobbies, mechanical |
| **Vacant square footage**, by period | Vacancy moves during a year and matters — see below |
| **Assignable space, by function** | Tenant / each program / administrative / shared lab |
| Floor plan or CAD file | Marked up if possible; a hand-marked PDF is fine |
| **Rent roll** | Tenant, suite, leased SF, term, monthly rent, start and end dates |
| **Lease schedule** | Including the YSU joint use agreement space |
| Effective dates | If space changed use during 2025, when |

### Four things to get right

**Vacant space is not a federal cost.** Under **2 CFR 200.465** the cost of
space is charged to the activity that uses it. Vacant space in a building held
for lease is a cost of the rental operation, not something federal awards
absorb. So vacant SF has to be identified, not folded into the program share.

**Common area is allocated, not assigned.** Corridors and restrooms are prorated
across the functional areas they serve, which is only possible once the
assignable areas are known.

**America Makes is both grantor and tenant.** They pay YBI $108,000 a year for
Boardman Street, and YBI separately expenses about $84,300 of Boardman Street
electric and gas. Occupancy costs recovered through that rent **cannot also be
loaded into an indirect pool charged to America Makes awards** — that is double
recovery, and 200.406 requires the rent to be treated as a credit against the
associated cost. Getting the Boardman Street square footage and its use split
right matters more than any other single building.

**Shared laboratory space needs usage, not just area.** If the additive
manufacturing lab serves several programs and tenants, square footage alone is
the wrong driver. A booking log, a scheduling calendar, or even a reasoned
estimate of machine hours by user is better evidence than dividing the room by
headcount.

### The YSU commitment

The twenty-year joint use agreement gives Youngstown State University the use of
"various space and use of additive manufacturing laboratory and testing
equipment". That space is committed to a third party. It needs to be identified
on the plan and excluded from the space available to federal programs, and the
agreement itself is the evidence.

### Acceptable formats

A marked-up floor plan and a current rent roll would resolve most of this. If no
measured plan exists, a schedule of rooms with approximate square footage and
who uses each is a workable substitute — an estimate with a documented basis is
evidence; an organisation-wide percentage with no basis is not.

### What it unlocks

- A defensible facilities carve-out, per building
- The Boardman Street double-recovery question, closed
- The 50/30/20 estimate either supported or corrected

## What the system will do with it

Both feed controls that have to pass before a rate can be sealed:

- **Asset register depreciation must reconcile to $850,382.89**, or produce an
  explicit bridge explaining the difference.
- **Every material asset must carry a funding and use status.** An asset with a
  blank funding source cannot contribute allowable depreciation.
- **Each facility-period space partition must sum to 100%** of allocable usable
  space, or explicitly account for what was excluded as common or vacant.
- **Every carve-out prints with a citation, an amount, a driver and an evidence
  grade** in the audit package.

Until these arrive, depreciation and occupancy stay at TEST ASSUMPTION, and the
system will refuse to seal a rate that rests on them.

1. **The fixed asset register**, in whatever form exists, even with funding

2. **The Tech Block Building 5 award file** — what funded $8.9M, and how much of

Those three narrow the modelled rate range more than everything else combined.

## Addendum — space and equipment, September 2026

The system now holds five buildings, a rent roll with a market rate beside
every actual charge, and an equipment register that records floor area and
hours of use. What it needs to fill them.

### Per building (five)

| | Why |
|---|---|
| Address, owned or leased, and the landlord if leased | A leased building's cost is rent; an owned one's is depreciation, taxes, insurance and maintenance. They allocate differently. |
| Usable and rentable square feet | Rentable carries the common-area load. The carve-out works in usable. |
| Year built and, for owned buildings, the depreciation schedule | 2 CFR 200.436 — and the funding source decides allowability, 200.436(b). |
| Annual lease cost, for leased buildings | 2 CFR 200.465. |

### Per space (the rent roll)

One row per suite, lab, office or common area, summing to the building's
usable area:

| | Why |
|---|---|
| Label, floor, usable square feet | The carve-out driver. |
| Use — tenant, programme, administrative, shared lab, common, vacant, committed | Tenant and vacant space never reach a federal pool. |
| Occupant, and months occupied in the year | A suite empty for half the year is half a suite. |
| What was charged for the year | The actual. |
| **A market rate per square foot, and where it came from** | The comparison. A rate needs a source — a broker's survey, an appraisal, a comparable lease — and the system refuses one without. |

### Per machine

| | Why |
|---|---|
| Description, serial, acquisition cost **gross of any reimbursement**, in-service date | 2 CFR 200.313(d)(1); the gross figure must predate the netting in Finding 2024-001. |
| Funding source | 200.436(b). Depreciation on federally funded assets is unallowable. |
| Which building and which space it stands in | So lab floor can be attributed. |
| **Footprint including clearance** | Programme equipment in a shared lab consumes space the carve-out would otherwise spread over everybody. |
| Access — free, subsidised, charged, internal | What the incubator gives away. |
| An hourly rate with a basis, where it is lent | To value it. |
| Usage log: who, which programme, how many hours, what they were charged | The unit of the subsidy, and a better lab driver than floor area alone. |

### What this does and does not support

The subsidy — market less actual, on space and equipment YBI owns — is
**mission value, not cost share**. Under 2 CFR 200.465 a less-than-arm's-length
rental is allowable only up to what ownership would have cost, so forgone rent
on your own building is not a cost you incurred. It is worth counting for the
board, the Form 990 narrative, the state, and for arguing a facilities
component on 2026 proposals. It does not go on a federal cost-share report,
and the system refuses to record it as one.

What **is** cost-share eligible: space, equipment use or services donated to
YBI by a third party (200.306(e)–(h)), and unrecovered indirect cost with the
awarding agency's prior written approval (200.306(c)).
