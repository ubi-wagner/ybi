## What we found

YBI bills America Makes labour at a **loaded rate** — about **1.77× what the
person actually costs** in wages and fringe. That extra is indirect recovery,
but it is *inside the labour line* and nothing says so. Meanwhile all four
agreements elect the **10% de minimis** indirect rate, which is charged
separately and openly.

So the numbers are broadly fine; **the disclosure is not.** That is the finding.

## Why it is still worth doing

**$203,790 a year.** A loaded labour rate recovers indirect only on labour. It
cannot touch subcontracts, consultants, materials or travel — where most of our
award spending sits. A proper rate reaches it, so **the recurring value is
roughly twenty times the one-off.**

## The catch, and it is the real negotiation

A contract ceiling caps direct *and* indirect together. Today's budgets give
almost all of it to direct. If NCDMM accepts our rate but leaves ceilings
unchanged, the direct budget shrinks by **$674,367 — about 28% less programme
delivered for the same money.**

So the ask is **accept the rate *and* move the ceilings.** Accepting the rate
quietly and delivering the original scope is where we are now, in a new costume.
