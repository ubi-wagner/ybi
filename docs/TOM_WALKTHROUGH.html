Today, in order

For Tom Metzinger · the 2025 cost allocation · one pass, about twenty minutes

Read this first. The year is already classified and the rate is already computed. So today is checking that it still holds, not building it. Every one of the five steps below is something you look at — if everything holds you will not write a single thing today. The only part of this page that changes the record is the section at the bottom, and only if step 1 or step 2 turns something up.

It says 100% · 757 of 757 The year is loaded and judged. Carry on to step 1.

The five steps

1 Do the books still agree with themselves?

Reconcile · Schedule A-1

Eleven points where the general ledger, the profit and loss, the balance sheet and the payroll register have to say the same thing.

You should see eleven ticks — all eleven tie.
If any is open stop here. A rate over books that do not agree is a rate over the wrong numbers, and the system will refuse to compute one anyway. Close the difference by naming the lines behind it, never by netting it.

2 Do the judgments still stand?

Classify

This is the real work, and it is the only step that needs your judgment. 757 groups were classified and recorded under your name, each with a written rationale. What has not happened is a person reading them and saying they are right.

You do not have to read all 757. They are ordered by money — the top twenty or thirty carry most of it. If they look right, the rest follow the same rules.

If they all stand nothing to do. Skip to step 4.
If any is wrong go to “If something has to change” at the bottom. Do not change it here first — the set is sealed, and the system will refuse you.

3 The seal

Rates

The seal is the assertion that the classifications were fixed before any rate existed — which is what lets you tell a reviewer the rate was not reverse-engineered. It is the one thing in this system that only means something because a person did it.

You should see it already sealed, in your name, over 757 judgments — and the buttons offered are Unseal… and Compute the rate. Nothing to do.
If you see a Seal decision set button the set is open. Finish step 2 first, then press it. Do not press it on a queue you have not read — that is the whole thing the seal is for.

4 The rate

Rates · then Review → Rate

The rate is already computed. Computing is arithmetic; the judgment was sealing, which is already done. You do not need to press anything here today.

You should see four rates: FRINGE, OVERHEAD, G&A and INDIRECT_COMBINED — and the basis recorded as POOL.
If you ever do recompute, the dropdown labelled Administrative labour defaults to “a cost objective” and that is not what this engagement settled on. Change it to “in the G&A pool (it is the indirect)” before pressing Compute the rate. It is worth about nine points of combined rate on exactly the same judgments.

5 Does the rate tie to what is underneath it?

Review → Rate

Nothing on this screen is calculated for display — every figure is read from the row it was recorded in. That is what makes it a workpaper.

You should see four pools at variance 0.00 and four anchor checks tying.
Read the line above the figures too. It should say that no 200.465 facilities carve-out is in this rate, because no building has measured square footage yet. That means the rate reads high — which is the honest direction to be wrong in, but it is the single largest open item and it is Kelly's square footage that closes it.

Certification — and the one thing you cannot do

You cannot certify, and that is correct

You have no timesheet and no “My effort” tab, because you are not on the 2025 payroll register — you are paid as Metz Consulting, LLC. There is no distribution under your name to sign, and the system will tell you so if you reach the screen by URL.

And you cannot sign for anybody else. 2 CFR 200.430(i) wants the record of the person whose effort it was. A controller signing forty-three certifications would produce forty-three signatures worth nothing.

So what is actually outstanding is this: 0 of 43 people have signed for their own effort. The whole $1.8m labour distribution is the controller's reconstruction, which nobody has yet read and affirmed.

That does not change a single figure in the rate. It changes whether the rate is usable, because 200.430(i) goes to the allowability of the entire direct labour charge. It is the largest thing between here and a defensible file, and it is a chasing job, not a clicking job.

Each of the forty-three signs their own by opening My effort, reading the statement, ticking “I have read the statement above” and pressing Sign certification. They can correct the reconstruction first if it is wrong. What you can do today is ask them — and the first thing they need is an account, which most of them do not have yet.

If something has to change

Only if step 1 or step 2 turned something up. In this order, and not out of it:

  • Unseal — Rates → Unseal…, with a written reason. This supersedes every rate computed against that seal. That is expected, not a problem.
  • Correct the judgment — Classify. Reclassifying supersedes rather than edits; the old judgment stays on the record with your reason beside it.
  • Re-seal over the corrected set.
  • Recompute — and choose “in the G&A pool” again (step 4).
  • Re-check step 5.
  • Tell whoever was quoted the old figure. The system cannot do this one.

Correct by superseding, never by editing. Nothing you do here removes anything from the record.

Five things only you can do

Everything else in this system is automated on purpose. These are not, because a machine doing them would destroy exactly what they are worth:

  • Sealing — it is the assertion that the rate was not reverse-engineered.
  • Saying a classification is right — step 2.
  • Naming a reconciling difference — a difference is closed by saying what it consists of, never by netting it away.
  • Anything that goes to a sponsor. Every restatement in the system is marked PROPOSED and stays that way until NCDMM says otherwise in writing.
  • Asking forty-three people to sign.
If a screen says something different from what this page says it will say, trust the screen and tell Eric — this page was written against the record as it stood, and the screen is reading it live.
