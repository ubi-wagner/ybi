Step 3 Tom · controller /rates

Sealed. The hash covers every live judgment in the set. Only now does computing become possible.

Step 3 — the judgment the whole system rests on. Sealing hashes every live classification and freezes the set. Only CONTROLLER may do it and nothing automated may do it at all: a script that sealed would put the machine's name on the assertion that the rate was not fitted to a target.

Afterwards a classification can only change by unsealing with a written reason, which supersedes any rate already computed. The card now reads from the record — it said “Before sealing” over a sealed set until this walk photographed it.

In parallel

In parallel Heidi · employee /timesheet

The pre-filled sheet, offered as a convenience. It says it is optional before it says anything else, and names the three complete answers. Nothing here has been adopted for anybody.

Running in parallel, at their own pace. The controller's reconstruction of a person's year, offered to the person whose work it was.

It says this is optional before it says anything else, and names three complete answers: type your own days and ignore it, adopt it and correct what is wrong, or leave it. A convenience that reads as an instruction is the one way this exercise produces forty-three signatures worth nothing.

2 CFR 200.430(i) does not require a contemporaneous record — it requires one that reflects the work performed, supported and reviewed after the fact. A reconstruction the person reads, corrects and signs meets that. One nobody ever saw does not, which is where 2025 has been sitting.

Certifying is a separate act on a separate screen. Adopting puts hours on a sheet; signing says the sheet is true. One button doing both would take a signature from somebody who had only meant to accept a starting point.

Nobody signs for anybody else. A project manager cannot sign on their team's behalf — v_certification_chase is a list to go and ask, never an action.
