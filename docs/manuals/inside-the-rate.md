# Inside the rate

What makes up the indirect rate, down to the transaction, and how to take any
part of it away as a file.

This is the answer to one question: **when America Makes asks what is in the
pool, where do you look.** The rate build-up workbook answers *how the rate
was struck* — pool over base, less what came out — which is what a reviewer
needs to reproduce the arithmetic and is not what you need walking into a
negotiation. This pane is the other half.

Nothing in here changes a figure. It is a read, from end to end: no screen in
it writes to the cost record, so the sealed rates stand exactly as they were
computed, whatever you open and whatever you download.

## Where it is

**`/rates` — the Rate tab, marked `7–9`.** The pane is at the foot of the
page, under the seal, the Compute button and the signature card.

It is also on **`/review/rate`**, the read-only workpaper, below the
build-up. It is the *same* pane in both places, not a second reading of it, so
a figure here and the same figure there cannot disagree.

The auditor sees the identical pane with no buttons on it. That is right —
reading the cost record is a grant and the figures are the record; sealing,
computing and certifying are yours.

## The four tabs

    Indirect, combined      what goes on an invoice — overhead plus G&A
    Overhead                occupancy and the facilities carve-out
    G&A                     general and administrative
    Fringe                  the six benefit accounts over the payroll register

Each tab is a whole rate: its own pool, its own carve-outs, its own base.
**Indirect, combined is not a third rate beside the other two** — it is the
overhead and G&A pools added together over the same base, which is why its
account count is the sum of theirs.

## The five sections, in the order they read

### The pool, as classified

Every ledger account judged into this pool, largest first, with the number of
groups and lines behind each. This is the figure a sponsor asks about, taken
apart: the accounts are what you talk about, not the total.

### What floor area drives

Only on Overhead and Indirect, combined, and only where there is something to
say. The square-footage carve-out may reach the cost square footage *causes*
and no more — so T1 access, telephone, insurance and equipment stay in the
pool however much of the estate is let. Open *Which accounts are the
exceptions, and why* for the four and the reason on each.

That part of the pool is the **floor under the overhead rate**. It is why the
rate cannot be carved to nothing, and it is worth saying out loud in a
negotiation.

### Carve-outs

What came out of the pool before the rate was struck, with the regulation
each cites — 2 CFR 200.465 for the let share of occupancy, 200.436(b) for
depreciation on a federally funded asset.

### Administrative labour in the pool

Only under the POOL basis. It says which objective it came from and which
basis the rate was computed on, because the same sealed judgments give two
different answers depending on that choice and the pane says which one you
are reading.

### The base

What the pool is spread over, read from the allocation the computation
recorded. The unit differs and it is not cosmetic:

- a **wage base** is one row per person — that is the payroll register, which
  is the whole point of the eleventh control;
- an **MTDC base** is one row per cost objective.

Printing people under objective headings would say the rate was taken over
the ledger's wage accounts, which is the other denominator and a different
number.

## The tick on every section

Every section carries its own footing line:

    n accounts · what they come to  ✓ ties to the rate

Both figures print — what the rows come to, and what the rate on file
carries — because a reader comparing a breakdown to a workpaper is comparing
two numbers and taking the tie on trust otherwise. **A breakdown that does not
add back to the figure on the workpaper is worse than no breakdown**, in a
negotiation most of all: a sponsor who adds the column up and gets a different
answer stops reading the rest.

Three states, not two:

    ✓ ties to the rate
          the rows add to the figure the rate carries

    ✗ against … on file — out by …
          they do not, and the difference prints, rather than the tie
          being taken on trust

    nothing to break the … on file into
          there is no breakdown to draw, and it names the figure that
          has none

The third is not a failure. A per-objective base exists only for Indirect,
combined — the allocation is recorded once, against the rate it was computed
on — so asking Overhead for one compares an empty table to the whole base. It
says so instead of printing a variance the width of the base.

## Down to the transaction

Click any account row. A drawer opens with:

- **the amount in the pool, the number of transactions and the number of
  payees**;
- **Where it went** — every payee in that account, largest first;
- **Every transaction** — the ledger lines themselves, two hundred at a time
  with *Show more* to the end, each carrying the date, the payee, the amount,
  the 990 function and the federal treatment;
- **The judgment these sit under** — the citation, the rationale and the
  grade somebody recorded. A transaction says what was spent; a sponsor asks
  why it is in this pool, and that is the answer.

## Three disclosures worth opening before a meeting

Each sits under a small grey line you click to expand.

    Which accounts are the exceptions, and why

## Taking any of it away as a file

Every section head carries **`CSV · Excel`**, and so does the drawer. There is
no "export everything" button to find — you take the level you are reading.

**Two formats because they are two jobs.** A CSV is pasted into a sponsor's
model; an .xlsx is filed as a workpaper. A figure that arrives in the wrong
one gets retyped, and that is how a number stops reconciling.

Ten things are exportable:

    The pool, as classified            every account in this rate's pools
    Carve-outs                         what came out, and the citation on each
    The base                           people or objectives, whichever this
                                       rate's base is
    What floor area drives             the four exceptions and the reason
    The estate                         square feet per building, by use
    The assets federal money bought    the 200.436(b) register
    Administrative labour in the pool  under the POOL basis
    Who was paid                       every payee in one account
    Every transaction in this account  the lines behind one account
    Every transaction in every pool    all of it, across every pool

Three things travel on the face of every file, so a CSV that has left the
screen still says what the screen said:

- **the footing line** — the rows, the total, and whether it ties;
- **the signature band** — `NOT CERTIFIED` with the reason, or who signed and
  when;
- **what is unfinished**, where anything is.

**The file carries every line; the screen carries a page.** A drawer showing
200 of 340 exports all 340, and the footing line on the file says *340 of
340*.

A section that has no breakdown to draw exports as a `NO DATA` file saying
what is missing, rather than a header over no rows. An empty CSV pasted into a
model gives a reader a header and no data and no way to tell which of the two
happened.

## What this pane does not do

- **It computes nothing.** Every figure is read from the register that
  already holds it — the gross from the pool balance, the carve-outs from the
  carve-out register, the driver split, the estate, the wage base, the MTDC
  base. A figure derived twice is one that can disagree with itself, and the
  workpaper would carry the version nobody can reproduce.
- **It writes nothing to the cost record.** No decision, no seal, no rate, no
  allocation. Opening it and exporting from it cannot move a sealed rate.
- **It records that you read it.** Opening a drawer is one act and taking a
  copy is another, and the register says which — a pane open is not an
  export, because an export means a file left the building.

## Two things to expect in a negotiation

**The combined rate is not the two component rates added up.** Overhead and
G&A are each their own pool over the base, rounded; the combined rate is the
*combined pool* over the same base. All three are right and the first two will
not sum to the third to the last decimal. Quote the **pools**, which add
exactly.

**The rate the papers carry is the rate they were measured on.** If the
record has since recomputed, the restatement screens and both sponsor papers
say so above their figures. A page printing one rate while the pane shows
another is not a contradiction to explain away — it is the notice doing its
job, and recomputing is your act, not the system's.
